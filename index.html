It sorts your library

Point Aniko at your anime folders. It reads every file name, works out the show, season and episode, and brings in covers, descriptions and episode titles.

It plays any file

Watch in the built-in player or in mpv. When a device can't play a file as it is, Aniko converts it while you watch.

It keeps your list

Finish an episode and your AniList progress updates. No account? Aniko keeps your list on your own computer instead.

The player

Press play. Aniko remembers the rest.

Every episode opens where you stopped, with the audio and subtitles you chose last time.

  • Resume to the second, in the built-in player and in mpv.
  • Your tracks, kept per show. Pick Japanese audio with full subtitles once.
  • Skip openings, endings and recaps by themselves, or with one button.
  • Up next and auto play at the end of each episode.
  • Picture in picture on the desktop, with the same controls.

Your devices

Start on the PC. Finish on the TV.

Aniko apps on your home network find each other. Turn on sharing on the computer that has the files, and its library shows up on the others, ready to play.

  • A TV app made for the remote, on Fire TV and Android TV.
  • Continue watching follows you from one screen to the next.
  • Wake a sleeping computer from the sofa. It stays awake while you watch.
  • Add a Raspberry Pi with mediaserver for a library that's always on.

Privacy

It stays in your home.

Aniko runs on your own computer. It has no account of its own, no ads and no tracking, and nothing on the internet can reach it.

The Aniko windowAlways. It proves itself to the app with a key made fresh each time it starts.
A browser on the same computerOnly if you turn that on.
Devices on your home networkOnly if you allow them, with a password if you like. They can't change folders or programs.
Your other Anikos and TVsOnly the ones you switch on, and only your library. Every request between them is signed.
Anything on the internetNever. Connections from outside your home are refused.

Questions

Is Aniko free?

Yes. It's free and open source under the GPL-3.0 licence, with no ads, no paid tier and no account of its own.

Does Aniko come with anime?

No. Aniko doesn't host or provide anything to watch. It organises and plays the files you already have, and keeps your list up to date.

Do I need an AniList account?

No. With an account, your lists, progress and scores stay in step with AniList. Without one, Aniko keeps your list on your computer.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes, in the phone's browser while you're at home. Turn on "Allow devices on my network" in Settings and open your computer's address: Aniko has a phone layout.

Why does Windows or macOS warn me the first time?

The apps aren't signed with a paid certificate, so Windows shows a SmartScreen notice and macOS asks you to choose Open Anyway once. The code is public, and every release is built from it on GitHub.

How do updates work?

Aniko shows an Update button on its Home page when a new version is out, and tells you what's new afterwards. New versions come out as betas first; you only get those if you turn on Beta updates.
